# Independent challenge to the integrated community release

Reviewed November 27, 2026 against branch baseline `ede1189`. This review traced the worktree and used synthetic local accounts and data. Production and real players were outside the test.

## Release judgment

The strongest addition is the short path from discussing an item to publishing a build another player can question. The item, champion, patch and author's reasoning travel together. Following that author, saving the thread, or recording what happened after trying the advice each gives the reader a distinct choice. This fits a social platform. A larger champion encyclopedia or five independent activity feeds would do little to improve that exchange.

A controlled pilot can test whether the exchange attracts independent helpers or potential duos. Success means someone else gives a helpful response, the author returns on a later day, or two willing players queue together again. Self-quotes, several visits by the same host, and clicks on an objective timeline do not establish those outcomes. Include the number of unanswered builds and follow-up questions when reporting the pilot.

## What the source supports

| Surface | Value | Limit to communicate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search and champion hubs | A player can search a champion or item, enter the associated hub, and retain a route back to the search. Community identities and Riot lookup records are separate result types. | The hub sample covers up to 20 public games for each of up to 300 recently refreshed profiles. It counts player appearances. An individual game can contribute several appearances. This local sample cannot establish a champion's global win rate. |
| Builds | The server checks the confirmed patch, champion, purchased item IDs and an optional keystone. The author supplies the rationale. The comparison and public thread can share a permalink. | A recognized item and keystone establish that they appear in the catalog. They do not establish the combination is optimal or legal to buy together for every champion. Two independently published guides in different rooms can legitimately disagree. |
| Native quotes | The new author writes fresh commentary. Storage holds a pointer to the original, which gets checked for the current reader before its excerpt is rendered. Commentary can survive withdrawal of the original. | A source author can withdraw their own post. They cannot withdraw someone else's words. A whole second quote graph, public quote counts and proactive pings would amplify attention before the team knows how much of it is welcome. |
| Profiles and progress | The public profile presents authored match conversations. The personal Playbook offers helpful answers and a route to recording a follow-up. | Public discussions can feature somebody else's match. Showing a discussion on a community profile means that person wrote it. A self-reported result after trying advice cannot establish causal improvement or a successful duo. |
| Objectives | An explicit link fetches selected team objective events from a completed public match. The projection uses times, team colors where known, and objective names. | The server still receives the larger Riot response containing participant and frame data. Only projected events belong in output, cache and diagnostics. A vendor failure, rate limit, or absent timeline cannot be reported as “no objectives.” |

## Material findings at first inspection

- **Malformed legacy build patches could crash every surface displaying one.** The new build attachment sent a nullable parsed patch into a helper that calls `.split()`. The earlier renderer guarded the input. Keep malformed historical records readable with an unknown-patch label. Guarding a fresh creation path alone does not protect old posts. [Build attachment](../../src/components/build-attachment.tsx) · [Patch comparison](../../src/lib/pure.ts)
- **A hidden verified player could still appear in broad community search.** The people result and champion sample applied viewer-specific exclusions. The neighboring cached Riot-player suggestions initially had no such check. Separate deliberate exact public Riot lookups from unsolicited name suggestions. Match on verified ownership so one person's unverified claim cannot hide an unrelated player. [Search query](../../src/lib/social.ts) · [Search routing](../../src/app/search/page.tsx)
- **A champion build could look missing immediately after publication.** A build started from an item was posted globally. The original champion-room Builds tab queried that room's posts, while its “Explore all” link led to every visible build for the champion. Keep the destination visible at publication and distinguish room posts and champion-wide results at reading time. Creating a second copy would fragment comments and withdrawal. [Room queries](../../src/lib/room-feed.ts) · [Champion page](../../src/app/rooms/[slug]/page.tsx)
- **The richest social seed has weak trust signals.** The author may quote their own build. A single person can generate comments, quotes and views before another player has responded. This can also be genuine follow-up. Count distinct non-author responses independently of self-updates. Use reports and human judgment before deciding to impose a per-source quote limit. [Quote controls](../../src/lib/quote-posts.ts)
- **Catalog freshness is a real write-time dependency.** A patch can roll while a player is researching and writing. Preserve the draft, explain the change, and revalidate item and rune choices. Reconfirming an old build should require a currently available loadout. A cell containing an old patch number is still valid historical context. [Build validation](../../src/lib/post-input.ts) · [Reconfirmation](../../src/lib/social.ts)

## Closing replies

An author-operated “Close replies” switch is a defensible finish line. It gives someone a way to stop an increasingly hostile conversation while preserving the replies they already received. Default to open. State clearly that the post stays public, existing replies remain visible, and quotations and reports have separate controls. A follower-only setting introduces hard questions over old replies, changing follow relationships, and who can answer during a cold start.

The write check needs to apply to direct replies, nested replies and One Fix. Concurrent closure and posting need a defined ordering. A viewer who opens a thread before closure must get clear feedback and keep their unsent wording. Turning replies off should never prevent reporting the original or an existing reply. A closed public help request should leave volunteer “needs an answer” queues if the volunteer cannot contribute. A locked thread is no reason to erase the private saved copy or already-delivered Activity link. [Comment write](../../src/lib/social.ts) · [Decision discovery](../../src/lib/decisions.ts)

## Capacity and operations

The objective path checks the bounded source record for the viewer before using its cache. It currently keeps up to 80 projected results for ten minutes per instance and coalesces up to four distinct in-flight loads there. An additional shared gate admits eight cache misses per minute across instances. These are operational limits, not a throughput benchmark. At eight unique timelines per minute for two minutes, up to 16 requests are spent out of the app's stated two-minute local budget of 98. Warm-cache requests still have a database cost for the visibility check. Record timeline throttling separately from stats lookup failures before raising either limit. One visitor viewing match history should cause zero timeline calls until they explicitly open a timeline. [Objective service](../../src/lib/objective-timeline.ts) · [Riot quota handling](../../src/lib/riot.ts)

The extra quote projection touches ordinary feed reads. Its original-post and original-author indexes support source lookup and withdrawal, and the normal feed limits apply first. The build index can still hydrate up to 100 posts with their interaction controls. Compare initial HTML transfer and mid-range phone responsiveness when the real build collection grows. Paginate that surface before increasing the limit. No new global message fan-out, trophy system, game archive or capture service is necessary to run this test.

## Changes and verification

The final code handles a missing legacy build patch, filters hidden verified owners before returning public player suggestions, and lists up to 12 visible builds for the champion directly on its hub. An item-origin build can now appear there under the same post ID. The updated patch-race fixture checks that a stale draft is rejected with a recoverable explanation. Closing replies serializes against comment creation, and reopening restores the normal write path. These changes address the concrete integration defects found during review.

At the release owner's request I also changed the three suggestion paths. The public unanswered list, personally matched questions, and empty-Following starter now skip closed requests. The active-conversations recommendation also skips closed threads, while the ordinary feed, post, and author history continue to surface them. A withdrawn response no longer disqualifies an otherwise unanswered starter. The personal tab now reads “Questions I can help with” and points a reader to other open questions when none match their preferences.

I independently ran seven focused suites covering quotes, timeline projection, search, champion hubs, profiles, decisions and build input. All 36 checks passed. After changing discovery I ran the decision, Following starter and home-discovery suites. All 12 checks passed; the decision suite is included in both runs. The fixtures covered source withdrawal, visibility after blocking and muting, a closed and reopened help request, and cache access after the viewer's permissions change.

I also used a separate Chromium journey against the local production bundle with guest, writer and reader sessions. Opening a room at its compose fragment exposed the actual writing field. Ordinary match detail showed no objective timeline until the visitor clicked. The timeline then displayed Blue team's dragon at 12:34 and Red team's Baron at 24:15, in chronological order, without player names or persistent Riot identifiers in that region. The author used Post options to close replies. A signed-out visitor could still read the existing comment, a new nested reply was declined, and reopening admitted a fresh reply. The fixture uses synthetic sessions, an isolated database and a simulated Riot response. Live Riot latency, quote abuse prevalence, and whether peers return to try a build remain unmeasured.
