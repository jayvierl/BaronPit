# Community Moderation — League of Legends Social App: Implementation Research

*Prepared 2026-11-25 (America/Chicago). Product assumed: text-first social platform — posts, comments, rooms per champion/topic, build sharing, duo/LFG finder, user-uploaded gameplay clips, profiles tied to real Riot IDs with rank + recent match history. Small independent team, bootstrapped. All web facts below are from the web index (search-result full text) unless noted; vendor prices were read from vendor/comparison pages surfaced 2026-11-25 — re-check vendor pricing pages before signing, prices move.*

## Summary — the 10 decisions that matter most

1. **Copy Riot's feedback loop, not Riot's scale.** Fast, specific, evidence-showing punishment is the single best-evidenced intervention: reform rose to ~92% when feedback arrived within 5–10 minutes, vs ~50% for an explanation alone and ~70% when chat-log evidence was included. Build: report → action → notify reporter ("your report led to…") → show offender the exact content + rule (a "reform card"). This is cheap and is Phase 1.
2. **Positive reinforcement works and is also cheap.** Riot Honor launch week: Verbal Abuse reports −41% normals / −17% ranked, Offensive Language −35%/−20%. Overwatch Endorsements+LFG: abusive-chat matches −26.4% Americas / −16.4% Korea, later claimed −40% toxicity overall. Build a lightweight endorsement ("good teammate / helpful builder") tied to visible profile + LFG filterability — with anti-trading rules.
3. **Pre-post nudges beat post-hoc bans for the persuadable majority.** Twitter RCT: reconsider prompt → 6% fewer offensive tweets, durably. Instagram: ~50% of warned comments edited/deleted. One classifier call before Publish gives you both.
4. **Do NOT build on Perspective API as your long-term core.** It is free and good at toxicity scoring, but multiple 2026 sources report Jigsaw sunsetting it after 31 Dec 2026, with quota-increase requests closed Feb 2026. Use it only behind an abstraction, or start on OpenAI Moderation (free) + Azure (paid, stable pricing).
5. **Buy moderation as one managed stack if you can afford ~$120–220/mo at 100k items; otherwise compose free APIs + your own queue.** Stream AI Moderation publishes the clearest all-in-one price: $2/1k text, $4/1k images, $6/1k images+CSAM, $0.80/min uploaded video, $100/mo free credit. The composed stack (OpenAI free + Azure ~$0.38/1k text) is near-$0 in API fees but you build queue, thresholds, appeals yourself.
6. **Clips are your highest legal risk, not text.** US law (18 USC §2258A, as expanded by the REPORT Act 2024) requires reporting apparent CSAM/enticement/trafficking to NCMEC CyberTipline once you have actual knowledge, and preserving reported material 1 year (raised from 90 days). You are *not* required to scan — but if you scan (you should, via hash-matching) you must have the reporting pipeline ready *before* the first match. Use a host/vendor that includes CSAM hash-matching (Stream images+CSAM, Hive/Deploy Thorn-backed, Thorn Safer directly, or Microsoft PhotoDNA — free to platforms, via NCMEC/Microsoft application).
7. **App stores are a hard launch gate, not a formality.** Apple Guideline 1.2 and Google Play UGC policy both require, verbatim in substance: filtering, in-app report, block, published contact info / terms prohibiting objectionable content, and *timely* action — Apple's rejection letters operationalise this as act within 24 hours. Parler was removed by Apple, Google *and* AWS in Jan 2021 for inadequate moderation.
8. **Riot ID linkage is your best anti-ban-evasion asset — use it as verified pseudonymity, not real-name.** Korea's real-name experiment reduced malicious comments only ~0.9 percentage points and was struck down in 2012. But a Riot account with history is expensive to replace: require Riot ID link + minimum account/rank/history threshold to post in LFG, create rooms, or upload clips; keep display names pseudonymous.
9. **Staff from queue maths, not a magic ratio.** No reliable published mod:user ratio exists; decentralised-social data shows 1:3,500 (worsening, IFTAS 2025) to 1:100–1:1,800 on well-run small servers. Formula + worked numbers in §3.2. Non-negotiable floor: never one person alone able to (or required to) handle urgent/CSAM reports.
10. **LFG needs its own safety surface.** Ban off-platform contact handles in listings, keep first contact in-app, one-tap block/report *from the listing*, no DMs from brand-new accounts, and explicitly ban boosting/account-sharing solicitation (Riot treats duo "hitchhiking" and account sharing as rank manipulation: 3-day → 14-day → permanent). Women are ~12% of LoL's surveyed player base and 59–77% in surveys report gender-based harassment / hiding their gender — design LFG so gender need never be disclosed to be safe.

## Findings

### 1. What Works — Evidence from Games & Platforms

#### 1.1 Riot's own systems — the closest evidence base you have

**Instant Feedback System (IFS) + reform cards.**
- Current Riot support docs: verified poor behaviour is punished **within 15 minutes of game end**; milder offences → chat restrictions; extreme behaviour can skip straight to 2-week or permanent suspension even with no prior offences; the reporter *sometimes* receives an Instant Feedback Report when their report caused a punishment; reports are anonymous to the reported player. Source: https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/penalties/instant-feedback-system-faq and https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/security/player-reporting-guide-and-faq?tref=ticketrelatedarticles — index, read 2026-11-25.
- The foundational research (Jeffrey "Lyte" Lin / Riot PB&J — Player Behavior & Justice — team, summarised from GDC/Nature coverage): ~50% of regularly-reported players did not re-offend if a moderator explained what they did wrong; **~70% if the explanation included evidence (chat logs)**; when ML automation cut feedback to **5–10 minutes after the offence, reform rate climbed to 92%**; verbal toxicity in ranked dropped ~40% after the automated system; extreme abuse (hate speech, sexism, racism, death threats) fell to ~2% of games. Lin's framing: ~1% of players are irredeemably toxic; design for the persuadable rest. Source: https://meta.discourse.org/t/measures-taken-against-bad-behaviour-in-league-of-legends/26084 — index (secondary summary of Riot/GDC/Nature material; treat exact % as Riot-reported, not independently audited).
- **Lesson for a third-party app:** you cannot punish in-game, but you *can* beat Riot on context: your offences happen *on your platform*, with the content preserved. Spec: (a) every sanction notice includes the exact post/comment, the rule cited, the sanction and duration, and the appeal link — no "you violated our policies" boilerplate alone; (b) target <15 min automated action on high-confidence violations, <1 h human action on severe reports during covered hours; (c) always close the loop with the reporter (actioned / no violation found — without disclosing the exact sanction if you prefer, but Riot's model and Overwatch's report-feedback emails both exist because unacknowledged reports teach users reporting is pointless and depress future report rates).

**Honor (positive reinforcement).**
- Riot's published launch-week results (Oct 2012, Lyte): Negative Attitude reports −29% normals / −11% ranked; Offensive Language −35% / −20%; **Verbal Abuse −41% / −17%**. Anti-abuse built in from day one: honours from strangers weighted above premade friends; pattern detection for trading — only 0.21% reported for consistent trading, 0.63% actively trying to trade, 0.01% spamming, all reset to zero. Modern Honor ties level to rewards and drops below baseline (starting level 2) after punishment. Sources: https://www.surrenderat20.net/2012/10/initial-honor-results.html ; https://bigshinyrobot.com/video-games/honor-in-online-gaming-the-league-of-legends-experiment/?cat=15 — index.
- **Lesson:** one week is launch novelty — do not promise −41%. But direction is consistent with Overwatch (below). Implementable version: endorsements only from users you've actually played/interacted with (co-room, duo completed, comment thread), max N/day, strangers > friends weighting, no self/premade-farming, endorsement level decays, and *gate privileges on it* (LFG visibility, room creation) rather than only cosmetics — privileges are what made Honor/Endorsements bite.

**Community Pact + graduated, severity-skipping ladder (current Riot model).**
- Riot's current Community Pact penalty menu: Warnings → Delays → Restrictions (feature-specific, e.g. chat) → Time-based Suspensions → Permanent Suspension → Hardware Bans; "penalties scale with severity and history… there's no 'three strikes' rule… permanent on first offence if serious enough"; retaliation is explicitly not a defence ("the other person started it" does not excuse your content). Source: https://www.riotgames.com/en/community-pact — index.
- **Lesson:** copy both rules verbatim in substance: severity can skip the ladder; retaliation is still a violation (judge each user's content on its own).

#### 1.2 FACEIT Minerva (with Google Cloud + Jigsaw)
- First 6 weeks live (2019, CS:GO text chat): analysed **200M+ messages, 7M marked toxic, 90,000 warnings, 20,000 bans**; toxic messages fell **20.13%** (2,280,769 in Aug → 1,821,723 in Sep); unique toxic senders −8%; matches affected by toxicity −15% in follow-up reporting; ~4% of all chat messages were toxic, affecting >40% of matches (prevalence baseline worth noting: a small % of messages poisons a large % of sessions). Decisions issued seconds after match end; warnings first, escalating severity for repeat flagging; months of training specifically to minimise false positives before full automation. Sources: https://www.gamesindustry.biz/new-community-management-ai-from-faceit-bans-20-000-players-in-trail-run ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/faceit-and-google-designed-admin-ai-bans-20-000-toxic-i-cs-go-i-players-in-six-weeks ; https://www.gamesindustry.biz/faceit-asking-players-to-help-train-minerva-ai-in-fairly-tackling-toxicity — index.
- **Lesson:** the transferable pattern is *warn-first automation with escalation memory*, and *train/tune on your own labelled data before you let the model ban anyone*. Minerva itself is not a product you can buy.

#### 1.4 Design-intervention research

- **Pre-post "reconsider" prompts — strongest cheap intervention.** Twitter RCT (Katsaros, Yang & Fratamico, ICWSM 2022): prompted users posted **6% fewer offensive tweets**, effect persisted in later weeks and reduced offensive replies received; ~9% didn't post and ~22% edited in a related summary; 3% edited to be *worse* (backlash exists). Instagram/Meta: **~50% of warned comments edited or deleted** (Meta testimony/announcements; ~1M warnings/day). OpenWeb (2020, via Prosocial Design Network): ~half revised or abandoned when prompted; +12.5% civil comments. Sources: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.00773 ; https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/national-bullying-prevention-month ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-117shrg54588/html/CHRG-117shrg54588.htm ; https://www.prosocialdesign.org/library/preliminary-flagging-before-posting — index. *Spec: trigger at your classifier's middle threshold; wording names the consequence ("this may be removed and count toward a strike") — NYU research on warnings found credibility of consequence is what makes warnings work, and effects decay after ~a week, so warnings must actually be followed by enforcement sometimes.*
- **Friction / slow modes:** Discord slowmode 5–15 s stops bot flooding with minimal human cost; use per-room slowmode as a *raid/incident lever* (mod-toggleable), plus permanent micro-friction for new accounts (see §2.3). No rigorous % effect found for slowmode on toxicity (as opposed to spam) — claim only spam/raid value.
- **Verified identity ≠ real names.** South Korea's real-name system (2007–2012): Korea Communications Commission found malicious comments fell only **0.9 percentage points** (13.9% → ~13%); heavy users unaffected/increased expletives in some analyses; Constitutional Court struck it down 23 Aug 2012; the identity databases it forced became a breach liability (Nate/Cyworld ~35M users' resident registration numbers exposed, 2011). Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/29/surprisingly-good-evidence-that-real-name-policies-fail-to-improve-comments/ ; https://catalystsforcollaboration.org/case-study-internet-identity-verification-system/ — index. *Your substitute: verified pseudonymity — Riot ID OAuth/link proves a real, aged game account with rank/history; display stays pseudonymous; sanction credibility comes from the cost of losing that linked identity, not from legal names.*
- **Pre- vs post-moderation:** Pre-moderation (hold everything) kills a social app's liveness and doesn't scale; pure post-moderation fails app-store "timely response" and lets harm be seen. Industry-converged answer is **risk-tiered hybrid** (Twitch/Stream model): auto-publish low-risk (established accounts, low scores), hold-for-review middle band + *all* content from probationary accounts in public rooms, auto-block high-confidence (slurs/CSAM/spam/known hashes). Reddit's Post Guidance test in r/AskReddit reduced would-be-removed content by 20% — pre-post guidance is pre-moderation's benefit without its queue.
- **Shadow moderation — use narrowly, disclose the policy.** Research consensus: visibility reduction without notice contains spam/bots effectively *because* the actor doesn't adapt, but perceived shadow-banning corrodes trust, fuels conspiracy folklore, disproportionately affects marginalised users in surveys, and prevents appeal/correction. Sources: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437221077174 ; https://news.ucalgary.ca/news/posting-shadows-impact-visibility-reduction-social-media — index. *Spec: shadow-limit only (a) spam/bot/ban-evasion signals, (b) time-boxed (e.g. 7–30 days), (c) disclosed as a technique in your transparency/policy pages even if individual timing isn't, and never as the sanction for a human's good-faith speech violation — those get a notice and appeal.*

#### 2.2 Image / video (clips), CSAM, and hosting

**Legal floor (US; get counsel — this is not legal advice):**
- **Duty to report, not (technically) to scan:** 18 USC §2258A requires electronic service providers to report *apparent* CSAM to NCMEC CyberTipline "as soon as reasonably possible" after obtaining actual knowledge. The **REPORT Act (2024)** added online enticement and child sex trafficking, raised preservation of reported material + associated records from 90 days to **1 year**, and raised penalties (up to ~$850k first / $1M subsequent for larger providers; willful failure is the catastrophic case). §2258A(f) says you need not affirmatively monitor — but the moment a user report, a moderator, or your own hash-match gives you knowledge, the clock and the preservation duty start. Sources: https://getstream.io/blog/content-moderation-api-comparison/ ; https://legalclarity.org/csam-cybersecurity-legal-obligations-and-detection/ ; https://dev.to/sentinelsafety/ncmec-mandatory-reporting-for-online-platforms-what-developers-need-to-know-4k74 — index.
- **Therefore, before clips launch you need, as code + process:** (1) a registered NCMEC CyberTipline reporting account and a named internal owner; (2) a report template capturing reporter identity, the visual depiction(s), user identifiers (username, email, IP), timestamps, geolocation if available; (3) an evidence vault: on a confirmed/suspected match, content is immediately invisible, original file + metadata frozen, access limited to the named owner, retained 1 year, never downloadable by general mods; (4) a rule that *volunteer room mods never see or handle suspected CSAM* — it escalates straight to the named owner.
- **Detection (best practice, industry standard):** hash-match every uploaded image and every sampled clip frame against known-CSAM hashes *before the content becomes visible*: Microsoft **PhotoDNA** (free to platforms, apply via Microsoft/NCMEC; robust to resize/re-encode, weaker to cropping), **Thorn Safer** (nonprofit, sales/contract; hash-matching + novel-CSAM classifiers; Thorn reports customers processed 415.4B files in 2025, found 5.3M suspected CSAM files — vendor/impact-report figure), **Hive/Deploy CSAM** (Thorn-backed, sales-priced), **Stream images+CSAM $6/1k** (cheapest self-serve published route), Cloudflare's CSAM scanning tool (free for cached content on Cloudflare plans), IWF lists for small platforms. Sources: https://dev2.mercdev.com/what-we-do/all-services/articles/content-moderation-tools-live-streaming-ugc-2026/ ; https://www.syntaqx.com/books/user-generated-platforms/child-safety — index. *Hash matching only catches known material; pair it with a general NSFW/sexual classifier (Azure/Hive/Stream) + human review of borderline minor-safety signals, and a "sexual content involving anyone who could be a minor → treat as CSAM queue" rule.*
- **Also hash-match for non-CSAM policy reuse:** perceptual hashing of *previously removed* gore/NSFW/spam images (open-source PDQ / TMK, Meta's Hasher-Matcher-Actioner is open source) stops re-upload whack-a-mole cheaply — Phase 2.

**Managed video hosts — what they do and don't do:**
- **Mux:** hosting/encoding/delivery + "Mux Robots" AI workflows including **content moderation at $0.001/sample** (pricing page assumes 10-second sampling for estimates); storage from $0.0024/min/mo, delivery 100k min/mo free then from $0.0008/min; Launch plan $20/mo for $100 credit. The moderation workflow is a generic classifier call — *it is not a CSAM/NCMEC pipeline*; you still own detection policy + reporting. Source: https://www.mux.com/pricing — index.
- **Cloudflare Stream:** $5 per 1,000 minutes stored + $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered, encoding free; no built-in content-moderation product for Stream itself (Cloudflare's separate CSAM tool covers cached content). Cheaper/predictable, but moderation is entirely yours to bolt on (sample frames → image API). Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/pricing/ — index.
- **Spec for clips (whichever host):** cap duration (e.g. 60–90 s) and size at launch; strip/re-encode all uploads (kills embedded payloads, normalises for frame sampling); sample frames at 1 frame / 2–5 s + thumbnail + poster frame → image moderation + CSAM hash; transcribe audio (host captions or ASR) → text moderation (slurs live in voice/audio, not just chat); **clips stay invisible until automated checks pass** (async pre-moderation is acceptable for clips in a way it isn't for comments — users tolerate a 30–120 s "processing" delay); no clip downloads / no external embed at launch (limits redistribution of anything that slips through).

#### 2.3 Spam, bots, and ban evasion

Layered, cheapest-first (Discord's published stack is the template):
1. **At signup:** email verification; Riot ID link required for full privileges (posting publicly, LFG, clips, room creation) — a linked account with real match history is your CAPTCHA-grade cost imposition; rate-limit signups per IP/device; honeypot fields + a CAPTCHA (Cloudflare Turnstile — free) only on risk signals, not for everyone.
2. **Probation (new-account restrictions):** e.g. first 24–72 h or until Riot-history check passes: cannot DM strangers, cannot post links/images/clips, limited posts/hour, all public posts pre-held or extra-scored, cannot create rooms, LFG listings invisible to others or badged "new". Discord analogue: verification level requiring account age + membership tenure before speaking. Publish the probation rules — secret probation reads as shadow-banning.
3. **Rate limits everywhere:** posts, comments, DMs, reports (report-spam is real), follows, LFG applications, username changes; per-account *and* per-IP/device; mention limits (Discord AutoMod blocks excessive unique mentions — copy: cap @-mentions per post, no @everyone for non-mods).
4. **Behavioural signals for evasion:** on ban, retain (hashed, access-controlled) ban-evasion signals: Riot ID (strongest — a banned Riot ID cannot simply re-link; a *new* Riot account has no history and fails your history thresholds), email hash, device fingerprint, IP history, payment ID if any. New account matching a banned Riot ID → auto-block link; matching weaker signals → auto-probation + mod alert, *not* auto-ban (shared IPs/dorms/dorm NATs are your core demographic — false positives will hit students hardest). Reddit's H2 2024 data shows why this matters: **ban evasion was 65.1% of all account sanctions** — plan for evasion to be your #1 sanction category, not an edge case. Source: https://boomreddit.com/blog/buy-reddit-comments/ (extracting Reddit Transparency Report H2 2024) — index, secondary.
5. **Spam economics:** no links until trust tier 2; link allowlist for known domains (youtube/twitch clips, op.gg etc.); duplicate-content detection (same text/link across rooms → auto-hold); slowmode + join-rate alarms per room (raid mode: one click → slowmode on, probation tightened, invites/links paused).

### 3. Policy & Operations Blueprint

#### 3.1 Minimum viable policy set for launch (documents, not vibes)

Publish *before* launch, in-app and on web:
1. **Community Guidelines** (one page, plain language, examples in LoL vocabulary): harassment/hate (incl. targeted slurs, sexual harassment), threats/doxxing (posting real-world info, incl. from Riot profiles), sexual content + absolute ban on sexual content involving minors, spam/scams (incl. account selling, boosting services — see §3.4), cheating/script promotion, impersonation (of Rioters/pros/streamers), self-harm content rules, illegal content. Riot's Community Pact (Play to Win / Play Fair / Play with Respect) is a good *tone* model and users already know it: https://www.riotgames.com/en/community-pact
2. **Terms of Service / User Policy** that (a) prohibit the same list (Google Play *requires* objectionable content be defined and prohibited in terms), (b) require acceptance *before* creating/uploading UGC (Google Play requirement), (c) grant you licence + removal rights, (d) state the sanctions ladder and appeal right.
3. **Enforcement / sanctions page** (the ladder below, with durations).
4. **Appeals page/process** (below).
5. **Child-safety statement + NCMEC process** (internal runbook; public page states zero tolerance + reporting to NCMEC).
6. **Privacy policy** covering Riot ID data, moderation logs, retention (incl. 1-year CSAM preservation exception), ban-evasion signals.
7. **Published contact information** (support/safety email in-app — Apple requirement) + a law-enforcement contact route.
8. **Room moderator code of conduct** (§3.3) and **Transparency page** (commit to first report within 6 months).

**Graduated sanctions ladder (spec — copy Riot/Xbox shape):**

| Step | Sanction | Typical trigger | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Nudge / warning with evidence** (no strike, or strike recorded) | First low-severity (profanity at someone, mild toxicity) | Reform-card format: content + rule quoted. Warnings only work if credible — record them and count them |
| 1 | **Content removal + formal warning (strike 1)** | Clear harassment, spam, first slur (context-dependent) | Reporter notified |
| 2 | **Mute / restriction 24 h–7 d** (can't post/comment/DM/LFG; can read) | Repeat within 90 d, or single moderate-severe | Feature-specific restriction beats full suspension (Riot chat-restriction model) — keeps user in ecosystem, removes the weapon |
| 3 | **Suspension 7–30 d** (whole account) | Further repeat, targeted harassment campaign, scam attempt | |
| 4 | **Permanent ban + Riot-ID blocklist** | Severe first offences skip here: CSAM (also NCMEC), credible threats, doxxing, sexual harassment of a minor, ban evasion, selling accounts/boosting operation | Severity-skipping is explicit in Riot's model — publish that there is no guaranteed 3-strikes path |
| Parallel | **Trust-tier demotion / quarantine** | Borderline repeat, mass-report signals not yet verified | Xbox "Avoid Me" analogue: reduced LFG/feed reach, pre-hold on posts, heals with clean period (e.g. 30–90 d) |

Rules that make ladders work (evidence-informed): strikes **decay** (e.g. expire after 90 clean days — Riot lets reputation "heal"; permanent records produce "nothing to lose" recidivism); every sanction states **what, which rule, how long, how to appeal**; sanctions are **per-content judged on its own merits** (retaliation still counts); **first severe ≠ first low** (skip logic above).

**Appeals design:** in-app appeal form on every sanction notice; one appeal per sanction; SLA: acknowledge immediately (automated), decide within 72 h (24 h for suspensions if you can); appeals reviewed by someone *other* than the original decider where staffing allows (at tiny scale: a second founder/mod, or re-review after 24 h cool-off); overturn restores content/account + removes strike; track overturn rate as a quality metric (benchmarks: Discord Q3 2022 reinstated 4.64% of appeals (1,334/28,736); Q2 2022 only 0.6% (733/122,714) — variance shows category mix matters; Reddit H2 2024 appeal success ~18.6% average across content/account appeals per secondary extraction). If your overturn rate is ~0%, your appeals are theatre and users will conclude so; if >30%, your first-line automation is miscalibrated. Sources: https://ptb.discord.com/blog/discord-transparency-report-q3-2022 ; https://discord.com:2083/blog/discord-transparency-report-q2-2022 ; https://www.joeloya.com/ — index.

#### 3.2 Human operations at small scale

**How much moderator time per 1,000 users? — honest answer: no trustworthy universal ratio exists; compute from your queue.**
- What is published: IFTAS 2025 Social Web Trust & Safety Report — average decentralised-social mod ratio **worsened to 1:3,500**, spam overtook CSAM as top concern, **1 in 5 mods/admins reported trauma or exhaustion**. Fediverse governance research: well-run small servers run **>1 mod per 1,000 members, lightest ~1:1,800, several 1:100**; Meta 2020 was ~1:250,000 MAU (15k mods / ~3.8B MAU across FB+IG) — irrelevant as a target, useful to show automation does the heavy lifting at scale. Stream's cost model assumes human review at ~187 items/hour (~19 s/item) and $29.20/h fully loaded, i.e. **~$0.16/item reviewed**, and that ~10% of content needing human eyes is a workable planning figure. Sources: https://about.iftas.org/2026/01/08/the-2025-social-web-trust-safety-report-is-here/ ; https://www.wrecka.ge/revealing-the-gifts/?ref=thenexusofprivacy.net ; https://getstream.io/blog/moderation-cost-at-scale/ — index.
- **Worked formula:** `mod-hours/day = (daily items × % human-reviewed × seconds-per-item / 3600) + reports + appeals`. Example at 10,000 DAU, 20,000 posts/comments/day: automation resolves 90%, humans see 2,000 × 25 s ≈ **14 mod-hours/day** — i.e. ~2 FTE-equivalent spread across timezones, *or* founders + 4–6 active community mods contributing 1–2 h/day each. At 1,000 DAU (~2,000 items/day) it's ~1.4 h/day — founder-doable. **Recompute monthly from actuals; staff to the queue SLA, not to user count.**
- **Queue triage (severity-first, never FIFO):** P0 — CSAM/minor safety, credible threats, doxxing, self-harm imminent: interrupt-driven, target <1 h, named owner only for CSAM. P1 — targeted harassment in progress, sexual harassment, scams in LFG, raids: <4 h covered hours. P2 — standard reports (toxicity, spam, NSFW): <24 h (also your Apple SLA). P3 — appeals, borderline, room disputes: <72 h. Within each tier, sort by (a) reporter trust + independent report count, (b) accused's strike history, (c) content reach (views so far) — a toxic post with 5,000 views outranks one with 3.
- **Moderator wellbeing (non-optional, cheap version):** NSFW/gore queues blurred by default with click-to-reveal (Community Sift and Hive both market mod-shielding blurring — require it in whatever queue you build/buy); no mod reviews graphic/minor-safety content except the named, consenting owner; rotation (max ~2 h/day on the worst queues, mix with community/facilitation work); hard rule that mods can skip any item without justification ("pass button"); weekly 30-min mod debrief; free resources: TSPA (Trust & Safety Professional Association) guidance. IFTAS's 1-in-5 trauma/exhaustion figure is your justification.
- **Community moderators (Reddit/Discord model) — when and how:** Recruit when a room sustains >~500 weekly actives or the P2 queue breaches SLA for 2 consecutive weeks. Vet: account age ≥6 months (on-platform or linked Riot history), zero strikes in 12 months, active *and* positively-endorsed in that specific room, written application + scenario quiz (5 sample posts → action + reasoning), trial period 30 days with limited powers (remove/hold + escalate only; no bans), 2FA mandatory. Empower with: room AutoMod-style rules UI, slowmode, pin/lock, user mute-in-room (max 7 d), everything logged in a mod-log visible to platform staff. Never give community mods: account bans, IP/device data, DMs of others, CSAM queue, appeals on their own actions. Pay in status/access/early features, not revenue share (avoids employment/Moderator-Code complications); publish a Mod Code of Conduct (no self-dealing, no modding disputes you're in, no leaking queues, confidentiality).

#### 3.3 Room/community moderation + rogue/inactive mods

- **Room charter at creation:** every room picks a topic, 2–3 named mods (or platform-default moderation until it appoints some), and accepts platform baseline rules as a floor — rooms may be *stricter*, never looser, than platform rules (a room cannot allow slurs or NSFW-with-minors-adjacent content "as its culture").
- **Tooling room mods need (build list):** mod queue filtered to their room; AutoMod-lite per room (keyword/regex lists, link policy, mention cap, new-member gate, slowmode presets, held-post approval — Reddit AutoModerator + Discord AutoMod feature sets, §1.3); mod log (immutable, platform-visible); escalation button that converts a room case into a platform case with context attached; anonymous-to-users mod actions (act as "Room Mod Team", individual identity visible to platform staff only — reduces retaliation against volunteers).
- **Rogue/inactive protocol (copy Reddit's, publish it in advance):** platform retains, in Terms + Mod Code: (1) **inactivity** — no mod action in a room for 30 days by *any* room mod → platform notice; 60 days → platform may appoint interim mods / open applications (Reddit's redditrequest uses ~60 days sitewide inactivity); (2) **rogue** — mod violating Mod Code (mass bans of critics, selling mod spots/promotion, disabling safety filters, harassing users) → platform may warn → suspend mod powers → remove, in that order *except* active harm (sabotage, CSAM mishandling) which allows immediate removal; Reddit's model: removal for Moderator Code of Conduct violations, top-mod removal process with notice + chance to respond, and the cautionary tale — Reddit's 2023 forced reopening/replacement of protesting mod teams (e.g. r/malefashionadvice) badly damaged trust, so *use removal powers rarely, document publicly when you do (a mod-log post in the room), and never replace a whole team without a stated rule violation*. Sources: https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/15484268024596-Can-mods-be-removed ; https://mods.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003669692-How-does-mod-removal-via-the-top-mod-removal-process-work ; https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/09/reddit-male-fashion-advice-mod-take-over-api-protest/84 — index.
- **Champion-main rooms specifically:** expect the known subreddit/Discord pathologies — OTP gatekeeping ("not a real X main"), build-shaming, rank-shaming, patch-day floods, pro-player drama brigades from other rooms. Mitigations: room rules templates addressing rank/OTP gatekeeping by name; cross-room raid signals shared at platform level (same accounts hitting multiple rooms in an hour → platform alert); "patch week" is a predictable surge — pre-schedule slowmode defaults + extra mod cover for 48 h after each LoL patch.

#### 3.4 Duo / LFG-specific safety

**Threat model (from gaming evidence + Riot rules):**
- **Boosting / account scams are your endemic fraud.** Riot: account sharing, paid boosting, and duo "hitchhiking" (queueing with a much stronger smurf/booster to climb) are rank manipulation; penalties 3-day → 14-day → permanent, LP/MMR reversal, ranked-rewards loss; account *buying* almost always ends in the seller reclaiming via support and the account being banned — Riot's own support page catalogues the scam patterns. Therefore: **ban boosting services, account selling/sharing, and paid-duo advertising in LFG and everywhere on-platform** (also protects you: facilitating ToS-violating services endangers any future Riot API/developer relationship — and your Riot ID integration presumably depends on Riot API terms compliance; verify your use against Riot's developer policies). Sources: https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/account/don-t-buy-accounts ; https://www.eloascend.com/blog/elo-boost-safety-risks (secondary summary of Riot penalty FAQs, checked against Riot pages Nov 2026 per that source) — index.
- **Off-platform migration is where harm happens:** the classic pattern is listing → "add me on Discord / send your op.gg / WhatsApp" → harassment, scams, sextortion off your moderation reach. Spec: **auto-filter contact handles (Discord tags, phone, email, social handles, link shorteners) in LFG listings and first-contact DMs**; keep negotiation in-app; display a standing warning in every LFG thread ("never share your Riot password; Riot staff never ask; report paid-boost offers").
- **Features (build list):** report + block *directly on the listing and on the application*, one tap, without needing to have chatted; block = they can't see/apply to your future listings and vice versa; LFG posts expire fast (2–4 h) and require rank/role/champion from the *linked Riot profile* (not self-typed — kills most rank-lying); show endorsement level + strike-free badge + account age on listings; limit simultaneous listings and applications (rate = spam/scam signal); no DMs from probationary accounts; DM request (not open inbox) from non-mutuals, with media/links disabled until accepted; a "duo completed → rate your duo" prompt that feeds endorsements *and* a private negative signal ("would not queue again" — Xbox-style, affects their LFG reach before it becomes a formal report).
- **Women and minors:** LoL's player base in widely-cited surveys is ~87% male / ~12% female, ~10% under 18 (Reddit-survey based, directional only — Riot does not publish official demographics). Reach3 survey (2021, via secondary reporting): 77% of women gamers experienced gender-specific discrimination; **59% hide their gender** (avoiding voice, neutral names). A 2026 study of women/trans/gender-diverse competitive gamers: ~87% reported gender-related harassment; top avoidances were voice-only-with-friends, hiding gender, neutral usernames. Design consequences: (a) **text-first is already your best safety feature — keep LFG fully usable without voice**, never require voice chat or a "voice verified" badge; (b) don't display gender anywhere by default and don't ask for it in onboarding; (c) if you ever add voice, voice-masking/modulation must be an option (evidence is about *avoidance behaviour*, not a controlled trial of masking — present it as user control, not a cure); (d) **women-only / affinity rooms:** allow them as *opt-in, mod-run rooms* with the same platform rules (Discord/Reddit precedent is that affinity spaces survive when platform-supported); the debate risk is verification — do **not** gender-verify users (intrusive, excludes trans users, creates a target list); gate by behaviour/endorsement and mod discretion instead. Sources: https://wegotthiscovered.com/gaming/59-women-hide-gender-playing-games-online-survey-finds/ ; https://ntu-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/2585670 ; https://headphonesaddict.com/league-of-legends-statistics/ — index.
- **Age gating:** League is ESRB T (13+) / PEGI 12 and Riot ToS requires 13+. For your app: **set minimum age 13 with self-declaration + Riot-account age signals; if you cannot do COPPA-compliant parental consent, you must exclude under-13s and act when you learn of one (account removal + data deletion)**. COPPA (US, updated rule fully applicable Apr 2026): verifiable parental consent before collecting personal info from under-13s, separate consent for third-party disclosure, written security + retention policies. UK: ICO Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code) applies to services *likely to be accessed by under-18s* — high-privacy defaults, geolocation off, no nudge techniques exploiting children — plus the Online Safety Act's age-assurance duties for harmful content. Practical spec for a small team: **13+ gate at signup; under-18 accounts get safe defaults automatically** (DM requests from adults off unless mutual-room, no LFG 1:1 with adults by default / LFG visible only within age band where feasible, media in DMs disabled, profile not searchable by contact info, no public location fields); **do not launch 18+ rooms/spaces until you have real age assurance** — a self-declared "18+" room full of 14-year-olds is worse than none. Sources: https://genkid.app/blog/kids-privacy-laws-worldwide ; https://www.privo.com/blog/what-is-the-uks-childrens-code?hs_amp=true ; https://nhimg.org/coppa-and-childrens-privacy-guide — index.

### 4. Metrics & Targets

#### 4.1 What to measure (definitions + why)

| Metric | Definition | Target / use |
|---|---|---|
| **Prevalence (the north star)** | % of *views* (or sampled items) that are violating, from a random-sample human-labelled audit (e.g. 500 random posts/week), not from reports | Only metric that tells you if the community is actually getting cleaner; reports alone conflate trust with incidence. YouTube's Violative View Rate is the model |
| **Report rate** | Reports / 1,000 posts; reports / 1,000 DAU | Rising early is *good* (trust); interpret with prevalence |
| **Action rate** | % of reports actioned | Benchmarks below; very low = users learn reporting is futile; ~100% = you're actioning junk |
| **Repeat-offender rate** | % of sanctioned users sanctioned again within 90 d (Riot's "reform rate" inverted) | Your ladder's report card; Riot's evidence implies fast+evidenced first sanctions should push reform toward 70%+ |
| **Time-to-action** | Median + p90, by priority tier | P0 <1 h, P1 <4 h, P2 <24 h (Apple), appeals <72 h |
| **Appeal overturn rate** | % appeals granted | Healthy band roughly 2–20% (benchmarks below) |
| **Automation precision** | % of auto-blocks overturned on review/appeal, sampled weekly per category | Gate for raising automation: don't auto-*ban* on a category until its sampled precision is very high (FACEIT trained months pre-automation) |
| **Ban-evasion share** | Evasion sanctions / all sanctions | Expect it to become #1 (Reddit: 65.1%); rising detection is good, rising *successful* evasion (prevalence audit) is bad |
| **LFG safety** | Reports per 100 LFG sessions; % listings filtered for contact info; block rate in LFG | Your highest-risk surface gets its own dashboard |
| **Mod health** | Queue hours/mod/week; % items passed/skipped; mod churn | Cap and rotate before burnout (IFTAS 1-in-5) |

#### 4.2 Published benchmarks to sanity-check against

- **Discord H2 2021:** 402,659 user reports in 6 months (excl. 35M one-click spam reports); **28% of reports actioned overall; Harassment & Bullying was the largest category but actioned at only 18%** (context/report quality) vs high rates for Child Safety etc. Q2 2022: 726,759 accounts disabled excl. **27.7M spam accounts** (spam dwarfs everything — expect the same shape); 122,714 appeals, 0.6% reinstated. Q3 2022: 134,663 reports, ~15% actioned (as extracted), 28,736 appeals, 4.64% reinstated. Sources: https://discord.com:2087/blog/discord-transparency-report-h2-2021 ; https://discord.com:2083/blog/discord-transparency-report-q2-2022 ; https://ptb.discord.com/blog/discord-transparency-report-q3-2022 — index.
- **Reddit H2 2024:** ~6B pieces of content in 6 months (per secondary extraction ~2.03B posts+comments in H2 — extraction sources disagree on the denominator; verify before external use); **2.66% of content removed (1.42% by mods, 1.24% by admins)**; 16.07M user reports, **9.3% actioned by admins**; spam = 63.2% of admin removals; **ban evasion = 65.1% of account sanctions**; 72.5% of mod removals by AutoModerator. Sources: https://www.joeloya.com/ ; https://boomreddit.com/blog/buy-reddit-comments/ ; https://www.infocrowdsourcing.com/statistics/reddit-statistics/ — index, secondary extractions of Reddit's report.
- **FACEIT/Minerva prevalence:** ~4% of chat messages toxic, touching >40% of matches — use as a prior: expect low-single-digit % of your comments to be violating, but a large % of *threads/rooms* to contain at least one.
- **Riot:** extreme abuse down to ~2% of games (Riot-reported). If your sampled prevalence of severe content is >5% of views, you are an outlier in the wrong direction.

*Sanity rule for launch targets: report-action rate 15–30%, content removal 1–3% of items, severe prevalence <1% of views by month 6 — if you're wildly outside these, investigate your definitions before celebrating or panicking.*

#### 4.3 Failure modes and cautionary tales

- **Under-moderation → platform death by gatekeeper (Parler, Jan 2021):** Apple gave 24 h to submit an adequate moderation plan after repeated Guideline 1.1/1.2 failures (violence-inciting content); plan judged inadequate ("a temporary task force is not sufficient") → removed; Google removed it the same weekend; AWS terminated hosting for having "no effective process" to identify/remove violent content. Lesson: your moderation *plan document* + demonstrated process is itself a compliance artefact — write it before you need it. Sources: https://www.lee.senate.gov/services/files/1ac980e8-3cd8-492f-8422-782390d5108d (Apple's letter to US Senate) ; https://appleinsider.com/articles/21/01/10/apple-purges-parler-from-app-store-after-24-hour-deadline-passes — index.
- **Under-moderation → community toxicity death spiral (Voat):** Reddit-clone that absorbed banned communities, positioned on near-zero moderation, became synonymous with extremist content, lost payment/hosting goodwill, and shut down 25 Dec 2020 (founder cited funding; the reputational trajectory preceded it). Lesson for rooms: one notorious unmoderated room defines your whole app to stores, press, and Riot. Source for shutdown date: https://wiki.archiveteam.org/index.php?title=Deathwatch&oldid=52295 — index (weak source; the Voat narrative is widely reported but treat specifics cautiously).
- **Over-/rigid moderation + cost → quiet shutdowns (Miiverse, PlayStation Communities):** Nintendo's heavily pre-moderated Miiverse (every post human/automatically screened, very restrictive) still closed Nov 2017 as Wii U/3DS wound down; Sony discontinued PS Communities in Apr 2021 (announced as a product decision, no moderation cause published). *Honest read: neither is proven to have died **because** of moderation* — the lesson is narrower: heavy pre-moderation is expensive forever and does not by itself guarantee a community survives; don't buy Miiverse-level friction expecting safety to be the product. (Dates via ArchiveTeam Deathwatch for Miiverse; PS Communities date was not re-verified in this pass — see Could Not Verify.)
- **Over-moderation failure mode (general, evidence-backed):** false positives on reclaimed slurs, dialect, and LoL slang are the documented weakness of every classifier in §2.1; Overwatch's endorsement-decay gating basic chat produced sustained backlash. Mitigations: middle-band = hold/nudge (not removal), fast appeals, per-category precision sampling, and a public "we got it wrong" fix path.
- **App-store requirements — the exact launch checklist:**
  - **Apple App Store Review Guideline 1.2** (current text): UGC/social apps "must include: a method for filtering objectionable material from being posted; a mechanism to report offensive content and timely responses to concerns; the ability to block abusive users; published contact information." Apps that become primarily pornographic / Chatroulette-style random chat / objectification / threats / bullying "may be removed without notice"; developer must remove violating content and demonstrate improvement or be removed. Apple's rejection letters add the operational glosses reviewers actually test: EULA with zero-tolerance language, flag on content, block that works, **act on reports within 24 hours (remove content + eject the user)**. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ ; https://asoitis.com/app-review/guideline-1-2-user-generated-content — index.
  - **Google Play UGC policy:** must require acceptance of terms before creating/uploading UGC; define + prohibit objectionable content in terms; provide **in-app reporting *and* blocking** of content and users (mandatory for public social apps and for 1:1 features like DMs); conduct "robust, effective, and ongoing" moderation proportionate to the UGC type; incidental sexual content hidden by default behind filters requiring ≥2 actions to disable, and children excluded from accessing it; safeguards so monetisation doesn't incentivise objectionable behaviour. Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937 — index.
  - *Build both stores' lists into your Phase-1 acceptance tests; screenshot the report/block/filter flows for the review submission notes.*
